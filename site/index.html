Telegraph Hackathon Season II · Evaluators and Apps & Agents · testnet

Security answers you can check before you trust them.

On Telegraph, agents pay specialist miners for security answers. Our tests show a paid answer can be confidently wrong: approving malware, citing advisories that don't apply, calling versions that never existed "Secure". AdversarialEval measures those answers and checks them against public sources before anyone acts on them.

The problem, measured

testnet, rules fixed before any paid call
Trusting the route
On 21 hard Solana cases, Telegraph's ranked answer, trusted as is, approved key-stealing malware 5 times out of 5. With our free check, nothing unsafe was approved. Report
Rank is not quality
Across three paid rounds, the best miner was right 83% of the time and the default route 21%. Report
Real competition
37 miners are listed for security review, but they pay out to 13 addresses, and two addresses run 26 of them. Report
Fluency is not truth
Judges that prefer the longer or more alarming answer score near a coin flip on our pairs; our evaluator scores 77.5% on an independent test.

How we work

Every test's rules and answer key are committed before the first paid call. Every payment is matched to the chain or the node's ledger. Every result file is fingerprinted and signed, and a public check fails if one changes.

We report what didn't work as plainly as what did: a strong AI model with web search matched our checked results, and our adapter missed its own 80% bar. What we don't claim.
