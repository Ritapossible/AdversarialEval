# Held-out failure types `cpu-v5-lofo-F4F6`: SECURITY_REVIEW

Date: 2026-11-14 · config as cpu-v3 (rank 8, lr 2e-4, 6 epochs) · seeds 7 and 13 ·
per-seed reports `2026-11-14-cpu-v5-lofo-F4F6-seed7.md`, `…-seed13.md`.

**Why:** a peer in the Evaluators track (Fexx, Discord #showcase) reported 94% on error kinds
their adapter trained on vs 61% on kinds it hadn't seen. Our validation mixes all ten
families, so it can't show that gap. This run removes two whole families from training,
F4 (context reversal) and F6 (false alarm), and tests on them.

Training: `runs/frozen/train-264.jsonl` minus every F4 and F6 pair = 218 pairs. Unseen-family
test: the 46 F4/F6 training pairs + the 11 F4/F6 validation pairs = **57 pairs**
(`runs/frozen/unseen-F4F6.jsonl`).

## Reading

- **No transfer to unseen failure types.** On context reversal the adapter is at chance;
  on false alarms it is far **below** chance (23–26%), consistently across seeds.
- **Why below chance:** without false-alarm pairs, every remaining family rewards the answer
  that finds the problem, so the model learns "the alarmist answer wins". The validation
  split shows the same thing: when the right verdict is "Secure", accuracy collapses to
  19–46%, while "Insecure" stays at 79%. This is the polarity shortcut from 11-11 in a new
  form; F6 pairs are what hold it back.
- **Implications:**
  1. Our 72–75% validation holds only for failure types we train on. Pool pairs from other
     contributors may use types we never wrote, so expect lower accuracy there. This
     reinforces the independent-test requirement before registration.
  2. "Safe code correctly called safe" (F6, F4-safe-context) is the most important
     coverage to grow, in training and in the independent test.
  3. Report in the write-up: unseen-type accuracy, not only the mixed validation number.
