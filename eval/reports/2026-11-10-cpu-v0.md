## Reading

- **On 39 held-out second-voice pairs the adapter scores 61.5%.** That's below the kit's 65% gate and the node's 70% floor, and well below what we'd need to beat the incumbent (about 73.4%). The 71.4% on 14 pairs was noise: one pair is worth 7 points there.
- **It underfits:** 81% on its own training data after 3 CPU epochs, with validation loss still falling (0.68 → 0.65). More epochs, a higher learning rate and more data are the obvious next steps (run cpu-v1).
- **It's weak exactly where the project says scorers are weak.** When the right verdict is *Secure* it gets 45%. False alarms (F6) and overconfident "Secure" answers (F5) are each at 40%. Those are the inversion families. This is the measurement gap AdversarialEval is meant to close, and these two families need the most data.
- **It isn't just measuring length.** Padding the rejected answer to 1.5× the chosen answer's length keeps 92% of its correct calls.
