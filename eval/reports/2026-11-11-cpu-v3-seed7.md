What this does and doesn't show:

- **No measurable gain.** The mean moved +1.7 points, which is about 1.5 pairs out of 87.
  That's well inside the seed spread (cpu-v2's seeds were 5.7 points apart). Hand-written
  went from 77% to 81% on the mean, but that's 3 pairs, all on seed 13.
- **Batch F didn't fix its targets.** Errors shared by both seeds: 13 of 87. Four are
  hand-written: `v-code-java-objectinputstream` (F5), `v-code-xxe-defusedxml` (F6),
  `v-code-csrf-bearer-api` (F4) and one F2 (`GHSA-9c47-m6qq-7p4h`). The Java-deserialisation
  and defusedxml cases are exactly what batch F's 12 pairs were written for, and both seeds
  still miss them. Twelve more pairs in a 264-pair set don't move a specific concept.
- **No shortcut regression.** The shortcut hasn't come back: F2-out stays above F2-in (73% vs 42%).
  It has tilted slightly the other way. F2 as a whole is still near chance (61%), which fits
  the 11-11 decision not to claim version reasoning.
- **The padding probe stays at 100%** on both seeds, so length isn't driving the preferences.
- **Not ready to register.** Both lower CI bounds (62–65%) are below the node's 70% floor,
  and this is still validation, not an independent test.

Conclusion: at this size, the targeted single-pair error-mining loop has hit diminishing
returns. The gains that would matter are independent data (U1 test split, U2 reviewed
pool rows, U3 real miner responses) and variance reduction (U8 seed merge). We aren't writing
more same-author pairs.
