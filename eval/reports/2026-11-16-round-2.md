# Round 2: cost per verified correct SECURITY_REVIEW answer (2026-11-16)

Mission 10 (Model Economy), route 1. This is the first full round under the pre-registered
[rule v2](../../bench/RULE-v2.md) (commit `9714331`, before any round-2 call), with 46 cases
picked by a fixed rule ([`bench/cases-v2.json`](../../bench/cases-v2.json)). Rounds 3 and 4 repeat
it on Nov 17 and 18, and the pooled report follows them. Read this one as a single day.

## Setup

- **Run:** 2026-11-16, 598 paid calls.
  - 46 cases × 13 targets: the 12 miners that took a canonical request in that morning's
    [audit](../../bench/out/round-2/audit.json), plus the auto-routed ask.
  - One attempt each, at most 4 miners at a time.
- **Payment:** x402 on Ethereum Sepolia, test USDC, payer `0xA0C8…6C1C`.
- **Raw data:** [`bench/out/round-2/`](../../bench/out/round-2/), holding the calls with receipts,
  the audit, the free baseline, the scored output, and the on-chain payments.
- **Reproduce:**
  ```
  python bench/score_v2.py bench/out/round-2
  python tests/test_reproduce.py
  ```

## Findings

**1. Nobody beats the free lookup on packages, and nobody reliably reads code.**

Twin discrimination (exploratory, not pre-registered) gives credit only when both halves of a pair
are right:

| Target | Package pairs (affected and latest both right) | Code pairs (vulnerable and fixed both right) |
|---|---:|---:|
| OSV lookup, free | 12 / 12 | 0 / 6 (abstains) |
| a1-preflight | 9 / 12 | **0 / 6** |
| telegraph-verity-intelligence | 0 / 12 | **2 / 6** |
| every other target | 0 / 12 | 0 / 6 |

- **a1-preflight**, the most accurate miner (75%):
  - It answered `Secure` on 11 of 12 code snippets, including SQL injection, command injection and
    pickle deserialization. Its 6/12 on code is what "always Secure" scores.
  - In round 1 it did flag the same SQL injection, so its code answers aren't stable.
  - Its Go and Maven misses come from querying a shortened package name. OSV then finds nothing,
    and it reports `Secure`.

**2. The ranking carries no information about accuracy.**
- Kendall's τ between node rank and accuracy is **+0.02** over the 11 ranked miners, about zero.
- The miners at ranks 1, 2 and 3 scored 0%, 6% and 3%.
- The most accurate miner is rank 7. It was unranked the day before.

**3. The default route sends everything to the rank 1 miner.**
- All 46 auto-routed calls went to `pr-guard-sentinel`, and 5 of 36 were correct (14%).
- Called directly, the same miner returned no valid verdict at all: 34 of its 46 calls were refused
  before payment, and 12 were delivered with `verdict: null`.
- Through the router it answered `Secure` 22 times and null 24 times. Its `Secure` answers are only
  right where the package is clean.

**4. Buyers pay for failures, including ones the response doesn't show.**

The payer made **560 transfers of $0.01 (=$5.60)** on-chain during the round
([`onchain-payments.json`](../../bench/out/round-2/onchain-payments.json)). Of 598 calls, only the
38 refused before payment (`503 could not route`) went unpaid. Of the 560 charged calls:

| Outcome | Calls | Paid |
|---|---:|---:|
| delivered with a valid verdict | 119 | $1.19 |
| delivered, but `verdict` null or invalid | 72 | $0.72 |
| miner error after payment (e.g. "Supply an exact npm target", "Use one exact Composer release") | 246 | $2.46 |
| timeout (`504` or no response in 90 s): **charged, with no receipt or settlement header** | 123 | $1.23 |

- The 123 timeouts are why the pre-registered header-based measure reads $4.37 against the true
  $5.60.
- Round 1 shows the same pattern: 91 calls, 85 transfers, 6 refusals.
- Telegraph's docs say settled-but-failed calls are credited to escrow. We found no public endpoint
  to confirm that.

**5. Specialists are listed on a general intent.**
- Eight of the 12 reachable miners failed on almost every case with input-shape errors. For example:
  - npm-only: "Supply an exact npm target";
  - Composer-only: "Use one exact Composer release";
  - JavaScript source only: "This miner reviews JavaScript source, not websites".
- Each is still charged at $0.01 a call.

**6. Unknown inputs are answered with confidence.**
- a1-preflight, the router and the free lookup all called a non-existent package and a
  non-existent version `Secure` with 0 vulnerabilities.
- Every other miner errored, which counts as abstaining.

**7. Wording changes answers.**
- Restating the same package as plain text or as a question changed a1-preflight's answer in 4 of 8
  probes.
- The canonical `target` field doesn't say which format miners expect.

## What this means for evaluation

- On `SECURITY_REVIEW_GENERAL`, a judge that rewards fluent, confident answers can't separate
  these miners.
- What separates them is checkable:
  - Is `verdict` present and valid?
  - Does it agree with the advisory record for the exact version?
  - Does the reviewer change its answer between a vulnerable snippet and its fix?
- Those are the failure modes our evaluation material targets: minimal pairs on version ranges,
  severities and code twins. Round 2's real answers will be turned into reviewed preference pairs
  (plan M7).

## Limits

- **Single day.** Rounds 3 and 4 measure stability; ranks already moved between Nov 15 and 16.
- **Small samples.** 36 answerable cases give wide intervals. Differences inside overlapping
  intervals are ties.
- **The baseline is the answer key.** Package ground truth comes from OSV, so the free lookup is
  right on packages by construction. Its role is to show what a buyer can get without paying, not to
  rank it as a reviewer.
- **Twin discrimination is exploratory.** It was added after seeing the data.
- **ID match (secondary) under-counts.** Cases list OSV's GHSA ids, and some miners cite the CVE
  alias. It is not used for any finding.
- **Testnet only.** One payer wallet, test USDC.
