# Audit and upgrade plan (2026-11-10)

A senior-engineer review of AdversarialEval after the first two training runs. It records
what's strong, what's weak, and the upgrade plan that follows from it. Progress on each
item is tracked in [`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) (Phase U).

**Verdict:** a strong idea with a credible execution start, but our evidence isn't yet
independent. Score: **7/10 now, 9/10 reachable by Dec 1.** It's a realistic contender for an
Evaluators delivery award. The excellence award needs proof of measurement improvement that
we don't have yet.

## Strengths

1. **It targets the right layer, and the opening is real.** The incumbent cleared the floor
   by one pair, and demand is growing.
2. **Competitors fail where we designed defences.** That author has now been rejected four
   times in a row at the remote check (13–53%). That's the distribution-shift failure:
   adapters learn their author's style instead of the task.
3. **The engineering is ahead of the field.**
   - The pipeline works end to end: verified data, the kit's training, export, and the
     node's Stage 1 check.
   - It found five kit bugs, two of which silently block every author
     ([`UPSTREAM_ISSUES.md`](UPSTREAM_ISSUES.md)).
4. **The data discipline is real.**
   - Advisory facts come from OSV, and trap ids are verified.
   - Every naive scorer stays ≤ 59%.
   - It has caught three authoring errors.

## Weaknesses, by risk

| # | Weakness | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | **The 76.9% is self-graded.** The training and validation "voices" were written by the same author (Claude). | The node tests rows from 8+ independent contributors. Our number is probably inflated, and we haven't measured the shift that matters. |
| W2 | **We optimise a different target than the node scores.** It checks agreement with *other contributors' labels* on a hidden sample of about 21–30 pairs, not "adversarial robustness". We haven't seen one real miner response. | An adapter specialised on our style may not win on generic pool pairs, and may not transfer to the actual miner output format. |
| W3 | **Part of the taxonomy needs knowledge a 150M text model doesn't have.** F1 (id validity) and F2 (version ranges) need the advisory database. | The model can only memorise advisories or learn plausibility. F2 is stuck at 67% on validation, so the pitch overclaims. |
| W4 | **"Adversarial" is a label, not a process.** These are static hard negatives written once. | Nothing targets the model's actual errors or iterates. |
| W5 | **The statistics are thin.** One hidden pair ≈ 4.8 points. Our interval is about ±13 points, and validation also picked the epoch. | Luck dominates the challenge outcome, and we have no clean test estimate. |
| W6 | **The scale is small and the data is unreviewed.** 134 pairs, all AI-drafted. | Pool rows need human review and honest provenance. |
| W7 | **The commercial story is thin** (25 points). | A better evaluator only matters if it changes which miners get chosen. That isn't shown yet. |

Learnable from text alone: **F3** (completeness), **F4** (context cues), **F6** (safe
patterns), **F7** (field consistency), **F9** (hedging), **F10** (known-bad fixes). They
need knowledge: **F1**, **F2**, and partly **F8** (severity). See the re-scoping in U7.

## Findings since the audit (2026-11-11)

- **W5 confirmed:** identical config, two seeds, 76.9% vs 87.2% on the same 39 validation pairs.
- **W1/W4 confirmed, concretely:** the minimal pairs from U5 exposed a **polarity shortcut**.
  The model trained on hand-written data scores 100% when the right answer is "affected" and
  0% when it's "patched", on both unseen training-package pairs and held-out packages. It
  prefers the vulnerable-sounding answer instead of comparing versions. The hand-written
  validation set reported 100% on F2 and hid this. Minimal pairs are now part of
  validation (48 held-out pairs from 13 disjoint packages), and every report breaks accuracy
  down by source and pair kind.
- **Next experiment:** cpu-v2 trains on all 252 pairs (balanced F2-in/F2-out minimal pairs)
  to test whether the shortcut breaks.

## What changes in how we report numbers

- Validation (`data/validation/`) may guide training decisions. It's labelled "validation"
  everywhere and is never called a test result.
- The headline number comes from `data/test/` only, written by independent authors, with
  its confidence interval and seed spread.
- Every claim in the write-up says which split it comes from and who wrote that split.
